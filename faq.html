Recovery FAQs

No judgment. Just practical information and a place to begin.

Getting started

How do I know if I need help with alcohol or drugs?

If substance use is affecting your health, relationships, work, finances, or sense of control, talking with a qualified healthcare professional can help you understand your options. You do not need to reach a crisis before seeking support.

Do I have to hit rock bottom before starting recovery?

No. You can seek help at any point. Recognizing a concern and asking for support are meaningful first steps.

What happens at my first AA or NA meeting?

Meetings vary, but usually include a welcome and people sharing their experience. You can listen without speaking. Look for open meetings if you're attending as a visitor.

Is recovery possible without religion?

Yes. There are faith-based and secular recovery pathways, including mutual-help groups, counseling, medications when appropriate, and other evidence-based approaches.

Is it safe to stop drinking suddenly?

Alcohol withdrawal can be dangerous for people who are physically dependent. Speak with a healthcare professional before stopping abruptly; severe withdrawal symptoms require emergency medical attention.

Life in ongoing recovery

Does a setback erase my progress?

No. A return to use deserves prompt attention and support, but it does not erase what you've learned. Reconnect with your support network and seek medical guidance when needed.

Do I need meetings forever?

People maintain recovery in different ways. Some value lifelong meetings; others combine professional care, peers, family, and healthy routines. Your support plan can evolve over time.

How can I support someone I love?

Listen without judgment, encourage professional and peer support, set healthy boundaries, and look after your own well-being. You cannot force another person's recovery.

Where can I find help right now?

Visit our recovery resources, FindTreatment.gov, or AA meeting finder. In the U.S., call or text 988 for a mental health crisis; call 911 for immediate danger.

These answers are general education, not individualized medical advice. If you need urgent help, contact local emergency services.
